Executive summary

Among 15,240 peptides, the anchor has a different set of close neighbors depending on how similarity is measured. At the 50th-neighbor cutoff, only one other peptide appears in all three neighborhoods. At the broader scale, 19 other peptides share the anchor’s cluster under all three descriptions.

Residue order makes the biggest difference to the shape of the local neighborhood. Only 7 of the 61 nearest residue-pair neighbors belong to the anchor’s broader residue-pair cluster, compared with 47 of 52 for properties and 46 of 53 for composition. Treat similarity as a description of the library. These comparisons do not establish shared antimicrobial activity.

How to read the differences

Properties groups residues into broad chemical classes, so chemically similar substitutions can preserve proximity. Composition counts each amino acid separately but discards its position. Residue pairs record adjacent amino acids, so rearranging a sequence can move it even when composition stays identical. The pair map captures only 3.2% of its descriptor variance. Apparent overlap on that map is especially unreliable as a measure of distance.

The analysis uses every unique sequence in the master score export. AVG identifies the anchor and plays no role in distance or cluster assignment. Properties use standardized length and fractions for six groups (AVILMFWY, FWY, KR, DE, STNQ, GP). All library sequences are length 15, so length contributes zero distance. Composition uses 20 residue fractions. Pair counts use 400 possible adjacent pairs and unit-length normalization. Each view uses Euclidean distance and k-means with 12 clusters, ten initializations and seed 42. Twelve is a fixed comparison scale; the data do not establish 12 biological families.
